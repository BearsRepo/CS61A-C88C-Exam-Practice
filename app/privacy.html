Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 15, 2026

CS 61A & C88C Exam Practice is an independent student project by Jacob Kao and Osbert Huang. It is not an official UC Berkeley, CS 61A or C88C service. This page explains what the site stores, where, and why.

In short

  • There are no accounts, names, emails or passwords.
  • Your answers, code, notes, annotations and exam history stay in your browser.
  • When you start practicing, your browser gets an anonymous ID. Under that ID the site records usage summaries: which questions you practiced, how they were graded, how long you spent, and which features you used. These help us improve the site and will power a future “My Progress” page.
  • The site also uses Cloudflare Web Analytics for overall visit counts, without cookies.

1. What stays in your browser

These are saved in your browser's local storage on this device, and are not sent to us:

  • Your answers (choices and typed answers) and the code you write or edit.
  • Notes, highlights, drawings, sticky notes and environment diagrams.
  • Practice sets, exams in progress, submitted exam attempts and their scores, and your Exam History.
  • Settings and the tutorial's progress.

Two more things are stored in your browser for the features below:

  • The anonymous session that keeps your anonymous ID.
  • A short queue of usage summaries that haven't been sent yet. It is kept for up to 30 days if you are offline. It contains only the summaries listed in section 3, plus a short checksum used to notice that an answer changed. The checksum is not the answer itself.

Clearing this site's data in your browser deletes all of the above from your device.

2. Your anonymous ID

The first time you go past the welcome page, the site signs your browser in to Supabase (our database provider) as an anonymous user. This gives your browser a random ID. No name, email, password or student ID is involved.

The ID is pseudonymous, not invisible. We don't know who you are, but the same ID is used for everything recorded from this browser. Each browser or device gets its own ID. If you clear the site's data, your browser gets a new ID next time.

3. Usage summaries we record

Under your anonymous ID, the site records:

  • Practice sets and exams. Whether it was Practice or Exam mode, full past exam or topic-based, and which past exam. Also the language (Python or Gleam), how many questions it had, when it started, when you last worked on it and when it finished, and whether it was finished, submitted, or discarded.
  • For each question part you worked on. The part's identifier (for example “Fall 2025 Midterm 2, Q6(b), part ii”), its source and topics, and whether it is graded automatically. Also the result:
    • correct or incorrect for automatically graded answers;
    • your own self-grade if you gave one;
    • “not an exact match”, “not graded”, “graded by course staff”, or unanswered.
    Practice parts also record how many times you pressed Check, the result of your first Check, and whether you opened the solution. For an exam, these results are sent once, when you submit it.
  • Estimated active time. Time on a question screen while the page is visible and you have clicked, typed, scrolled or touched within the last minute.
  • Active days. The (Pacific time) dates on which you did something meaningful, such as starting a set, answering, checking or submitting.
  • Feature use. How many times you ran code with Run Code on each question, and whether you opened Python Tutor, a walkthrough video, an exam review or the environment diagram workspace.

We do not record the text of your answers, your code, your notes or annotations, your full exam history, names, emails, student IDs or official grades. We also don't record IP addresses in these usage summaries.

All of these summaries come from your own browser and describe practice on this site. They are not grades, and they don't predict official exam results.

4. How we use it

We use the usage summaries to:

  • understand how the site is used (for example, how many students practice each week, which topics are practiced most, and where students most often make mistakes);
  • fix problems and improve the site;
  • build a future “My Progress” page that shows you your own summaries.

We don't sell this data and don't use it for advertising.

5. Who can see it

  • You: your browser can read only its own summaries, nobody else's.
  • Other students: they can never see your summaries. The database enforces this.
  • The site's maintainers (Jacob Kao and Osbert Huang): they can see all summaries through the Supabase dashboard. Summaries are linked to anonymous IDs only.
  • Supabase: stores the data on our behalf as our service provider.

6. Services this site uses

Like any website, the services below receive technical information when your browser connects to them, such as your IP address, browser type and the time of the request. They process it under their own privacy policies.

  • GitHub Pages hosts the site. (GitHub privacy statement)
  • Cloudflare Web Analytics counts visits and page views in aggregate, without cookies, on the published site. (Cloudflare privacy policy)
  • Supabase provides the anonymous sign-in and the database for the usage summaries. Supabase's own service logs may include connection details such as IP address and browser information. (Supabase privacy policy)
  • jsDelivr serves the Python runtime (Pyodide) the first time you use Run Code on a Python question. Your code runs inside your browser and is not sent to jsDelivr.

Some links open other sites, which have their own policies:

  • Python Tutor: opens pythontutor.com with that question's code, including the answers you filled in.
  • Walkthrough videos: open YouTube.
  • Give Feedback: opens a Google Form.

7. How long it is kept

  • Usage summaries: nothing is deleted automatically, and that includes summaries for unfinished sessions. Summaries stay until the maintainers delete them or the project is shut down.
  • Backups and logs: copies may remain in Supabase's backups or logs for a period set by Supabase.
  • Unsent summaries: summaries still in your browser's queue are dropped after 30 days if they can't be sent.

8. Your choices

  • No opt-out yet: the site doesn't currently have a setting to turn off the usage summaries, or a page to delete your anonymous account.
  • Clearing site data: clearing this site's data in your browser removes everything stored on your device and gives you a new anonymous ID. Summaries already recorded under your old ID stay in the database, but they are no longer connected to your browser.
  • Asking us to delete your summaries: use the feedback form. Because the data is anonymous, we can only find your summaries if you include your anonymous ID. Ask in the form and we'll explain how to find it.

9. Changes

If what the site records changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.

10. Contact

Questions or requests: the feedback form.
